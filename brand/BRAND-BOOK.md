# Vanta Brand Book

## The operating system for how Vanta appears, speaks, moves, and feels

Vanta is a local AI operator: observant, private, decisive, and calm under pressure. This
book protects that promise across product, web, clothing, print, spaces, and the small
moments in between. It is a working source of truth, not a frozen presentation.

> **North star:** Observe. Interface. Execute.

## Use this book

- Start with the foundation and the relevant touchpoint chapter.
- Apply the **must** rules. Use the **may** rules only when they improve clarity or trust.
- Log a decision before creating a new recurring pattern, color, name, component, or
  physical object.
- Treat a marked proposal as a testable direction—not a settled fact about the product.

### Decision status

| Marker | Meaning | May ship? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Confirmed** | Carried from Brand Book v1.0 or verified in the product. | Yes, within the stated rules. |
| **Direction** | The recommended system expansion in this book. | Yes for an owned pilot; record results. |
| **Validate** | Needs a legal, technical, accessibility, or production check. | No for broad release until checked. |

### How this stays alive

1. Propose a change in the decision log with the problem, affected surfaces, and owner.
2. Test it in at least one real, difficult application—not on a blank brand board.
3. Check accessibility, licensing, reproduction, and operational fit.
4. Approve, revise, or retire it. Update the version and changelog in the same change.

The book begins deliberately constrained. Constraints are how the brand remains legible as
Vanta gains products and audiences.

# 1. Foundation

## 1.1 Brand promise — **Confirmed**

**Your work stays close. Your operator stays ready.**

Vanta does not perform intelligence. It makes competent action feel already present: it
reads the environment, translates complexity into clear decisions, and acts precisely when
useful.

## 1.2 Positioning — **Direction**

For people doing consequential work on their own machines, Vanta is the local AI operator
that turns dense context into clear, controllable action. Unlike theatrical assistants or
opaque cloud automations, it keeps the work near the user and makes its boundaries visible.

This is positioning guidance, not a product-security claim. Any claim about local execution,
privacy, retention, permissions, or model behavior must be verified against the released
product before publication.

## 1.3 Brand idea — **Confirmed**

### The Raven Operator

The raven signifies perception, memory, local intelligence, and controlled action. It is not
a playful mascot. It watches first and moves only when useful.

| Move | User experience | Brand expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observe | Vanta establishes context before requesting work. | Quiet framing, patient space, factual status. |
| Interface | Vanta makes choices and constraints understandable. | Clear hierarchy, labels at the edge, one primary action. |
| Execute | Vanta acts with explicit scope and a useful receipt. | Precise confirmation, restrained signal, no victory lap. |

## 1.4 Personality — **Confirmed**

| We are | It looks and sounds like | We are not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calm | unhurried, spacious, matter-of-fact | frantic, bubbly, over-animated |
| Capable | prepared for real work, specific about outcomes | speculative, showy, magical |
| Local | grounded in the user’s device and control | invasive, surveillant, disembodied |
| Mysterious | a little unknown, with depth and restraint | ominous, hostile, occult cosplay |
| Precise | one useful accent and one clear next step | decorative, noisy, needlessly clever |

## 1.5 Brand boundaries — **Confirmed + Direction**

Vanta must never make users feel watched. “Observe” describes Vanta’s method, not a claim
of constant surveillance. In product copy, say what Vanta can access, why, and what it will
do next. Never imply access, certainty, autonomy, or protection beyond the real product.

Avoid cute-assistant language, sci-fi prophecy, hype, false certainty, and surveillance
language. Replace “I’m watching your files” with “I can review the files you select.”

## 1.6 Brand architecture — **Direction**

Use a simple endorsed system until evidence demands more complexity.

```text
Vanta                         master brand / wordmark
├── Vanta Desktop              product descriptor
├── Vanta CLI                  product descriptor
├── Vanta Skills               capability descriptor
├── Vanta Agents               capability descriptor
└── Vanta [partner integration]  descriptive, never a new logo
```

- Write the master brand as **Vanta** in sentence case; reserve **VANTA** for the wordmark
  artwork and deliberate display treatment.
- Use descriptive names before invented sub-brand names. Recognition should build in one
  place.
- Do not create a product logo, mascot, or accent color for a feature. Use the Vanta system
  plus a descriptive label.
- **Validate:** product, integration, and feature names before release for trademark,
  domain, social-handle, localization, and confusingly similar-use risks.

# 2. Verbal language

## 2.1 Voice

Vanta speaks with economical confidence. Lead with what happened or what matters; name the
boundary; then make the next action evident. A short answer is a feature when the work is
clear. Detail is available when uncertainty, risk, or control requires it.

| Principle | Do | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct | “Ready when you are.” | “Hey! I’m super excited to help!” |
| Grounded | “Runs locally. Stays private.” | “Your data is totally safe with us.” |
| Capable | “I found the conflict and resolved it.” | “I worked my magic.” |
| Calm | “Nothing requires your attention.” | “URGENT!!! Take action now!” |
| Honest | “I need permission to continue.” | “Done” before the result is verified. |

## 2.2 Copy grammar — **Direction**

- Prefer active verbs: review, compare, prepare, connect, run, explain, save.
- Prefer observable nouns: task, file, permission, workspace, result, receipt, boundary.
- Use first person only when it identifies an action Vanta actually took or needs to take.
- State uncertainty plainly: “I found two likely matches.”
- Place the important verb first in controls: **Review changes**, **Allow access**,
  **Open receipt**.
- Use sentence case for product UI, headings, buttons, and error messages. Reserve all caps
  for short metadata such as `STATE: READY` or `BOUNDARY: LOCAL DEVICE`.

## 2.3 Message patterns

| Moment | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ready | status + available next action | “Local operator ready. Review today’s handoff.” |
| Permission | action + boundary + choice | “Allow Vanta to read this folder for this task?” |
| In progress | active verb + scope | “Comparing 12 files in this workspace.” |
| Complete | outcome + receipt | “Conflict resolved. View the change summary.” |
| Needs attention | fact + consequence + recovery | “This connection expired. Reconnect to continue.” |
| Empty | explain the value of the first action | “Add a workspace to give this task context.” |

## 2.4 Tagline hierarchy — **Confirmed + Direction**

| Priority | Line | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | **Operate locally.** | Product marketing, launch, environmental copy. |
| Supporting | Private agents. Clear boundaries. Real work. | Secondary lockups, decks, product narrative. |
| Method | Observe. Interface. Execute. | Internal culture, editorial framing, motion sequence. |

Do not stack all three lines on a single small surface. One thought per moment is enough.

# 3. Identity system

## 3.1 Equity to preserve — **Confirmed**

This is an evolutionary expansion of Brand Book v1.0. Preserve the assets that make the
current identity recognizable:

- the Vanta name and formal wordmark;
- the raven as a true, non-humanoid animal and symbol of the operator;
- the `>_` terminal signal and operator mark for constrained spaces;
- Vanta Black, Bone White / Ink, and functional Signal Violet;
- editorial serif paired with disciplined interface sans and system mono;
- monumental asymmetry, archival print texture, and large quiet fields;
- a single violet point of attention rather than a violet “look.”

## 3.2 Mark family — **Confirmed + Validate**

| Asset | Primary role | Minimum use | Do not |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wordmark | formal identification | 120 px digital width | typeset it as a substitute for supplied art |
| Raven image mark | hero, campaign, culture | high-resolution or vector master | use as a tiny app icon or a playful badge |
| Operator mark | compact product and utility identity | 24 px digital | redraw, stretch, or decorate it |
| Terminal signal `>_` | state, entry point, small directional cue | text-equivalent only where it remains legible | make it a repeated decorative pattern |

Clear space for every mark is at least the cap height of the **V** in the wordmark. Never
crowd a mark with navigation, partner identities, borders, or copy. Use approved source
files only.

**Validate before production:** create an asset package with SVG, outlined PDF, transparent
PNG at 1×/2×/4×, one-color black/white variants, favicon/app-icon masters, and a
hand-cleaned vector or high-resolution 1-bit raven engraving. This book specifies behavior;
it does not replace final trademark artwork.

The current vector source for the compact operator mark is in
`assets/marks/operator-signal-violet.svg`, with `-bone` and `-black` one-color variants.
Usage, minimum-size, and clear-space rules live in `assets/marks/README.md`. **Validate:**
clear the geometry for trademark use and test its line weight in embroidery and small icons
before treating it as final legal artwork.

## 3.3 Mark misuse

Never:

- alter proportions, add outlines, shadows, gradients, or glows;
- use the raven with humanoid body parts, clothing, armor, hands, or a comic expression;
- build a symmetrical esports-style crest around the raven;
- use violet as a large decorative fill behind a mark;
- place a busy image behind a mark without a tested, absolute-contrast field;
- make a feature-level logo from the raven, mark, or terminal signal.

## 3.4 Color — **Confirmed**

The palette has three colors. No drift.

| Name | Value | Role | Contrast guidance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vanta Black | `#050507` | field, primary surface, dark text | use with Bone for body copy |
| Bone White / Ink | `#F4EFE3` | primary text on black, light field, paper stock | use with Black for body copy |
| Signal Violet | `#7C3AED` | selection, focus, live state, cursor, small rule | not body text on Black |

The existing core pairs calculate as follows: Bone on Black is **17.75:1**; Violet on Black
is **3.57:1**; Violet on Bone is **4.97:1**. Therefore, use Bone/Black pairs for normal body
text; Violet on Black may identify a non-text UI state or large text; Violet on Bone may be
used for normal text only after the exact rendered size and weight are checked. Do not use
color as the sole indicator of state.

### Color behavior

- Target composition: **82% Black / 16% Bone / 2% Violet**.
- Violet means something: active, selected, focused, recording, connected, or a machine
  attention point. Pair it with a label or shape when meaning matters.
- Use opacity and tonal layering of Black and Bone for surface separation; do not introduce
  a new hue to solve hierarchy.
- No violet gradients, ambient glow, long text passages, or full-screen decorative fills.
- **Validate:** CMYK conversions, uncoated/coated proofs, embroidery thread, vinyl, and
  screen-print ink against physical samples. Hex values are not print specifications.

## 3.5 Token seed — **Direction**

The following is the starting contract for digital systems. Primitive tokens are raw
materials. Semantic and component tokens are the only values a UI should consume. Add
platform transforms later; do not let individual screens invent near-matches.

```json
{
  "color": {
    "primitive": {
      "black": { "$value": "#050507", "$type": "color" },
      "bone": { "$value": "#F4EFE3", "$type": "color" },
      "violet": { "$value": "#7C3AED", "$type": "color" }
    },
    "semantic": {
      "canvas": { "$value": "{color.primitive.black}", "$type": "color" },
      "surface": { "$value": "{color.primitive.black}", "$type": "color" },
      "text.primary": { "$value": "{color.primitive.bone}", "$type": "color" },
      "text.inverse": { "$value": "{color.primitive.black}", "$type": "color" },
      "action.primary": { "$value": "{color.primitive.violet}", "$type": "color" },
      "focus.ring": { "$value": "{color.primitive.violet}", "$type": "color" }
    }
  },
  "space": {
    "1": { "$value": "4px", "$type": "dimension" },
    "2": { "$value": "8px", "$type": "dimension" },
    "3": { "$value": "12px", "$type": "dimension" },
    "4": { "$value": "16px", "$type": "dimension" },
    "6": { "$value": "24px", "$type": "dimension" },
    "8": { "$value": "32px", "$type": "dimension" },
    "12": { "$value": "48px", "$type": "dimension" },
    "16": { "$value": "64px", "$type": "dimension" }
  },
  "radius": {
    "none": { "$value": "0px", "$type": "dimension" },
    "control": { "$value": "4px", "$type": "dimension" },
    "container": { "$value": "8px", "$type": "dimension" },
    "search": { "$value": "999px", "$type": "dimension" }
  }
}
```

Component examples: `button.primary.background` references `color.semantic.action.primary`;
`button.primary.label` references `color.semantic.text.primary`. Components must not consume
the primitive tier directly.

## 3.6 Typography — **Direction + Validate**

| Job | Typeface | Expression | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display | Sentient | editorial authority, myth, confidence | hero titles and rare editorial moments |
| Interface | Switzer | clear, neutral, disciplined | UI, navigation, long digital reading |
| System | SF Mono / platform monospace stack | status, metadata, commands | short labels and code-like information |

Brand Book v1.0 specified Noto Serif Display, Inter Display, and DejaVu Sans Mono. On
2026-07-22 the current marketing website was designated the visual north star, promoting its
Sentient / Switzer / platform-mono stack into the living system. Keep the earlier stack as a
licensed fallback until font packaging and redistribution are verified.

Use the existing scale as the floor: Display 01 `56/58`, Display 02 `38/42`, Heading
`22/28`, Body `11/17`, Label `8/12` (size/leading in points for the source book). For
digital product, do not use the 8 px label as functional text. Establish accessible web
roles during implementation: 16 px minimum default body text, 12 px minimum metadata when
essential, and at least 44 × 44 CSS px hit targets for touch controls.

- Display serif is a rare signal, not a UI default.
- Interface sans does most work. Keep line lengths readable and hierarchy visible.
- Mono denotes state, boundaries, timestamps, paths, commands, and receipts—not flavor text.
- Use tabular figures for dates, run time, counts, prices, and comparison tables.
- **Validate:** desktop/webfont licenses and redistribution terms before embedding or
  packaging typefaces. Define legal fallbacks before launch.

## 3.7 Composition and graphic devices — **Confirmed + Direction**

Vanta compositions are monumental, asymmetric, and still. Use one dominant subject, a large
area of quiet, and technical labels anchored to edges. Text may overlap imagery only when
contrast stays absolute.

Approved devices are operator brackets, a target reticle, terminal cursor, a thin signal
rule, and structured system metadata. Each must communicate identity, state, location,
sequence, or focus. A graphic device without a job is decoration; remove it.

Digital layout defaults:

- 4 px base spacing; use the token scale, not arbitrary gaps.
- 12-column desktop content grid, 6-column tablet grid, 4-column mobile grid.
- Keep a bounded reading column for dense copy; let the raven, hero title, or data view take
  the wide field—not all three at once.
- Prefer flat surfaces, thin dividers, and a single raised or inset moment only when it
  explains hierarchy.
- Reveal advanced controls progressively. The system should absorb complexity before the
  user has to confront it.

### Marketing-site north star — **Confirmed direction, 2026-07-22**

The current marketing site at `https://vanta.theft.studio/` is the visual north star for
website and documentation surfaces. Its grammar is flatter and more editorial than default
Docusaurus UI:

- **0 px** for structural edges and text actions;
- **4 px** for buttons, pagination, code blocks, and compact controls;
- **8 px** for true containers such as cards and data tables;
- **fully rounded only by exception**, currently the global search field and circular status
  indicators;
- thin Bone rules and quiet fields establish grouping before card backgrounds;
- Sentient leads display hierarchy, Switzer carries interface/reading text, and platform mono
  carries system state.

The portable Docusaurus override is stored at `brand/docusaurus-north-star.css`. The full
website theme source is not present in this workbench snapshot, so that stylesheet must be
integrated and verified in the complete website checkout before the hosted docs can change.

## 3.8 Imagery and illustration — **Confirmed**

| Element | Required behavior | Never |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject | real raven anatomy and silhouette | humanoid, armored, clothed, hand-like raven |
| Rendering | high-contrast engraving, halftone, xerographic grain, archival print texture | glossy 3D, synthetic chrome, airbrushed cyberpunk |
| Light | bone highlights cutting through deep black | multicolor neon lighting |
| Accent | one violet eye or interface point | multiple competing violet glows |
| Crop | still, monumental, often asymmetric | mascot badge or comic action pose |

Texture belongs in distinct, controllable layers. Never bake grain into small UI assets,
functional text, icons, or source logos. Use photography only when it shows real work,
material, people, or environments with the same quiet, high-contrast discipline.

## 3.9 Iconography, data, motion, and sound — **Direction**

### Icons

- Use a simple, geometric, mostly outline system with a consistent artboard and stroke.
- Start at a 24 px artboard with a 1.5–2 px stroke; test 16 px before approving.
- Use icon + visible label for unfamiliar or consequential actions. Never rely on a raven
  pictogram as a generic action icon.
- Use `>_` only for operator-related entry points or terminal-state context.

### Data and diagrams

Data views are evidence, not ornament. Use Bone for primary values, reduced-contrast Bone
for comparison, and Violet only for the selected series, active filter, threshold, or user
attention point. Label units, time range, source, and uncertainty. Never use a violet rainbow
to differentiate categories.

### Motion

Motion should clarify a state change, not simulate intelligence. Default to a short,
mechanical ease: 120–180 ms for interface feedback and 200–320 ms for contextual panels.
Use opacity, one-axis translation, cursor blink, or a single signal sweep. No constant
ambient movement, particle fields, elastic bounce, or theatrical “thinking” loops. Respect
`prefers-reduced-motion`; state must remain understandable with all motion removed.

### Sound

Vanta’s sound is quiet, brief, and opt-in. A successful completion may use a single dry,
low-volume two-note confirmation; a required decision may use a clear, non-alarming cue.
Never autoplay branded music, use surveillance-like effects, or communicate a critical state
with sound alone. **Validate** sound in shared offices, screen readers, and OS sound settings.

# 4. Digital language

## 4.1 Web

The website should feel like a calm field report, not a sci-fi launch sequence. Begin with
the visitor’s question and the proof needed to answer it. Build content before ornamental
screen composition.

### Page grammar

| Page | Job | Required elements | Visual emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Home | establish promise and next action | clear value, product proof, boundaries, CTA | monumental raven or product field; one violet signal |
| Product | explain a capability without mystery | task, how it works, control, receipt | real UI and annotated states |
| Trust / privacy | make constraints understandable | plain-language boundaries, current policy links, limitations | calm document rhythm; no raven required |
| Docs | enable action | task-first navigation, copyable steps, version/date | high legibility; mono for commands only |
| Download / onboarding | get to a safe first success | platform facts, requirements, recovery help | direct path; no competing campaign noise |
| Journal / stories | make a point of view durable | byline, date, readable measure, sources | serif title with restrained editorial image |

### Web rules

- One primary CTA per viewport. A secondary action must be visibly quieter.
- Put the concrete proof near the claim: actual workflow, explanation, limitations, or
  independent evidence. Do not let mood substitute for proof.
- Use real product UI screenshots where a user needs to evaluate capability; label mockups
  clearly when they are not a released state.
- Make pages responsive and keyboard-operable from their semantic baseline. Avoid horizontal
  scroll, hover-only content, text embedded in images, and cursor-dependent interactions.
- The raven earns a hero moment; it does not need to appear on every page.

## 4.2 Product app

The app is the most consequential expression of the brand. Its feeling of calm must come
from legible state, good defaults, and reliable recovery—not from darkness alone.

### Surface model

| Layer | Purpose | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canvas | stable working field | Vanta Black, generous quiet, Bone text |
| Navigation | recognition and orientation | persistent labels, modest active Violet indicator |
| Task surface | conversation, plans, results, receipts | flat grouping, clear chronology, visible scope |
| Inspector / details | deeper context on demand | progressive disclosure; no modal for ordinary reading |
| Decision moment | permission, destructive action, ambiguity | explicit boundary, consequences, primary recovery path |
| System status | local state, model, connection, execution | mono label plus plain-language explanation |

### Component grammar

| Component | Default | State behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Button | Bone label, clear verb, rectangular/minimally rounded | Violet signals the primary action or keyboard focus, never every button |
| Text link | descriptive Bone text with visible focus | never “click here”; underline or another non-color affordance on hover/focus |
| Input | persistent label, clear value, no ornamental chrome | error names the problem and recovery; focus is visible |
| Menu | recognized options, grouped by task | keyboard navigable; current state has text and indicator |
| Card / panel | used only to group a real unit of work | do not card every paragraph or status item |
| Toast | brief, non-critical confirmation | must not be sole delivery for failure, approval, or irreversible result |
| Modal / dialog | exceptional interruption | focus trap, Escape behavior where safe, focus returns to trigger |
| Empty state | explains value of first action | one concrete next step; no cute illustration filler |

### Work-loop rules

1. **Orientation:** show what workspace, task, and boundary the user is in.
2. **Intent:** let the user choose a recognizable action or state a request.
3. **Scope:** before consequential work, show what will be read, changed, sent, or run.
4. **Progress:** name the active work and its current scope; do not simulate deliberation.
5. **Receipt:** make the result, affected items, and reversal/review path accessible.

Every high-impact interaction needs a keyboard path, visible focus, and a plain-language
state that does not depend on color or motion. Use native controls before custom ARIA.

## 4.3 States and severity — **Direction**

The three-color core is not a complete semantic severity palette. Until a validated status
system exists, use language, iconography, layout, and optional native platform conventions to
communicate information. Do not silently invent “Vanta red” or “Vanta green.”

| State | Primary expression | Violet role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Selected / focused | label, shape, focus ring | allowed |
| Ready / local | plain-language status | small signal allowed |
| Progress | verb, scope, elapsed time if helpful | cursor or limited active point |
| Success | outcome and receipt | optional small confirmation |
| Warning / risk | explicit consequence and decision | not sufficient by itself |
| Error / blocked | factual error and recovery | avoid decorative use |

**Validate:** define severity colors only with accessibility tests, light/dark behavior,
non-color cues, and documented product semantics. The brand’s restraint is more important
than an arbitrary rainbow.

## 4.4 Accessibility baseline — **Direction**

Target WCAG AA as the floor. For every shipped UI:

- use semantic HTML/native platform controls first, then ARIA only where necessary;
- meet 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text and non-text UI indicators;
- give every interactive control a keyboard path and a visible focus state;
- provide a real label for every input; placeholder text is not a label;
- keep focus order logical; trap and restore focus for dialogs;
- support reduced motion, zoom, system font scaling, and high-contrast needs;
- test with keyboard-only use and a screen reader on the target platform.

The static color-ratio math in this book does not prove a released interface is accessible.
Rendered screens, states, and assistive-technology behavior still require testing.

# 5. Campaign, social, and editorial language

## 5.1 Campaign system

Every campaign begins with one humanly useful claim and one proof. Raven imagery may set the
atmosphere; it cannot carry the argument alone.

**Recommended structure:**

```text
Situation / friction
→ Vanta’s specific operating advantage
→ visible proof or workflow
→ one next action
```

Use a single bold display line, edge-anchored metadata, and one meaningful Violet detail. A
campaign should be identifiable with the logo covered.

## 5.2 Social

| Format | System behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Product clip | real task, bounded before/after, captions, clear result |
| Release note | concise outcome, who benefits, link to evidence or docs |
| Editorial raven | use sparingly to frame a point of view, not as daily filler |
| Carousel | one idea per panel; body copy at usable size; no dense screenshot mosaics |
| Community reply | direct, human, specific; do not role-play the raven or overpromise |

Never use rapid strobe, unreadable animated type, contextless “AI is magic” clips, or a
high-frequency posting style that contradicts calm competence.

## 5.3 Decks, documents, and email

- Let one conclusion lead each slide or page; put supporting data in a second layer.
- Use the display serif for the first thought, Interface for all working content, and Mono
  for version, source, date, and operating status.
- Preserve generous margins and a stable page grid. Avoid the “black rectangle with tiny
  white text” failure mode in printable documents.
- In email, prioritize readability and delivery over theatrical styling. Use a plain,
  trustworthy sender name and an accessible HTML/text baseline.

# 6. Physical language

Physical Vanta should feel collected, durable, and useful: an archival field kit rather than
conference swag. Material truth matters more than novelty.

## 6.1 Apparel and clothing — **Direction**

| Item | Recommended expression | Production guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operator tee | Bone or Black heavyweight cotton; small operator mark at chest; large raven engraving on back or interior neck message | one print location can be enough; avoid giant Violet fields |
| Overshirt / chore jacket | Black washed canvas or twill; tonal wordmark or small embroidered `>_`; utility label inside | fit, mobility, wash durability, inclusive size range |
| Cap / beanie | Black with small Bone or tonal operator mark | embroidery proof required; no mascot patch |
| Field vest / crew layer | only for real event/production roles; clear role label and restrained mark | visibility/safety requirements outrank brand styling |
| Notebook sleeve / tote | Bone or Black canvas; simple wordmark, edge label, or signal rule | test ink rub, seam strength, and legibility at distance |

Apparel must be comfortable, durable, easy-care, and respectful across body sizes. A garment
should earn its existence as something someone wants to wear even when they are not
representing the company. Avoid novelty costumes, faux-military styling, raven talons, or
uncomfortable “uniform” requirements.

### Apparel art direction

- Use one true raven engraving, a wordmark, an operator mark, or system metadata—not all at
  once.
- Prefer water-based or discharge ink on appropriate fabric, low-gloss embroidery, woven
  labels, and tonal treatments. Avoid plastisol-heavy, glossy, distressed-for-its-own-sake,
  or neon effects.
- Use Signal Violet as a small stitch, eye, inside label, barcode-like rule, or activation
  mark. It should feel discovered, not shouted.
- **Validate:** fabric sourcing, labor standards, local labeling requirements, colorfastness,
  thread/ink matching, and wash samples before ordering.

## 6.2 Packaging and shipping — **Direction**

Vanta does not require elaborate packaging for ordinary shipments. When a physical kit is
useful, make it reusable, recyclable where local infrastructure supports it, and easy to
open without destroying the experience.

| Layer | Expression |
| --- | --- |
| Shipping carton | uncoated Bone kraft-adjacent stock or Black single-color print; shipping/legal data stays functional |
| Inner wrap | Black tissue or paper band with a thin Violet signal rule; no purple floodcoat |
| Insert | one durable field card: what this is, why it exists, how to use it |
| Label | mono metadata: contents, date, version, source, batch if relevant |

Do not use the raven as a seal that makes a package look ceremonial or mysterious. Product
and regulatory information must remain more prominent than brand atmosphere.

## 6.3 Stationery and working objects — **Direction**

- Business cards: Bone stock, Black type, a precise mark; use the reverse for one useful line
  or contact method. Keep titles and abbreviations consistent.
- Letterhead: quiet wordmark and contact information; no full raven watermark behind copy.
- Notebooks: Black cover, small wordmark or operator mark, Bone pages, modest metadata line.
- Stickers: use the operator mark, `>_`, wordmark, or a single approved raven crop. Create
  a clear small-size proof before release.
- Badges: identify the person and role first. Brand second. Never trade legibility for mood.

## 6.4 Events, environments, and wayfinding — **Direction**

Spaces should be easier to enter, navigate, hear, and leave because Vanta is present.

| Surface | Role | System behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry | identify and orient | wordmark, clear welcome, accessible route information |
| Stage / demo | support attention | one large statement or product surface; no busy raven collage |
| Wayfinding | make movement obvious | high-contrast labels, simple arrows, distance-tested type |
| Work table | make interaction easy | labeled stations, power, tidy materials, clear staff roles |
| Lounge / hospitality | make the brand tactile and calm | durable Black/Bone materials, warm but not theatrical light |

Design for legibility, visibility, durability, local codes, mobility devices, quiet needs,
and sensory comfort. Avoid dark-on-dark signs, decorative haze, loud sound beds, steep
contrast changes, or a raven prop that turns a work event into a costume set.

## 6.5 Vehicles and equipment — **Direction**

If Vanta uses vehicles or equipment cases, use a highly legible wordmark, a plain-language
service or purpose, and one operator mark. Design at road distance and under poor light.
Respect safety regulations, surface curvature, maintenance cycles, and removal/replacement
costs. Simplicity rules the road.

# 7. Production and implementation

## 7.1 Critical application test set

Before locking a new identity element, test it in the following real applications:

1. desktop app shell at normal and 200% zoom;
2. mobile web page on a narrow viewport;
3. docs page with a long code sample and an error state;
4. social post with captions and no sound;
5. uncoated paper card and black garment print;
6. small sticker/app icon at its actual production size;
7. accessible permission or destructive-action flow.

If the system fails one of these, revise the system rather than making a private exception.

## 7.2 Preflight checklist

### Digital

- [ ] Content uses the verbal rules and makes no unverified privacy/security claim.
- [ ] Semantic tokens—not raw color values—are used in components.
- [ ] Focus, hover, selected, disabled, loading, error, and success states are specified.
- [ ] Keyboard, screen-reader, zoom, reduced-motion, and contrast checks are recorded.
- [ ] A screenshot or recording shows the real feature state, not only a mockup.
- [ ] Fonts, imagery, code, and icons have appropriate licenses and attribution.

### Print and objects

## 8.4 Brand review questions

Before approving a new expression, ask:

1. Does it make Vanta calmer, clearer, and more controllable—or merely more dramatic?
2. Could someone recognize it with the logo covered?
3. Does Violet communicate a meaningful state?
4. Does it preserve a quiet field around one important thing?
5. Is the raven anatomically true and treated as a symbol, not a mascot?
6. Can a person using a keyboard, screen reader, zoom, or reduced motion understand it?
7. Does it survive its actual material, size, distance, and maintenance context?
8. Is the required proof, license, and claim verification attached?

If the answer to any applicable question is no, the work is not ready to standardize.

# Appendix A. Quick-reference do / do not

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| use real raven anatomy | turn the raven into a humanoid or mascot |
| use Violet as a functional signal | add colors or use Violet as decoration |
| favor still, monumental crops | use neon cyberpunk glow or busy collages |
| use print grain in large image layers | bake noise into UI, icons, or small marks |
| leave large fields of silence | fill every space with UI debris |
| make boundaries legible | imply surveillance, certainty, or unlimited autonomy |
| show real product proof | rely on mystique or fictional capabilities |
| create useful, durable physical items | create disposable branded clutter |
